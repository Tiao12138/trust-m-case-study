Case Study

Trust-M

More accessible information about the services that help immigrants integrate into life in Espoo.

In cooperation with Emilia Ahlroos, Mirte van der Nat & Kalle Nikula

This project supported the City of Espoo and the Trust-M research team in designing a new AI-assisted service that helps immigrants find integration services in Espoo. The outcome is a set of service recommendations grounded in the lived experience of immigrant mothers — recommendations Trust-M can use to validate ideas while designing their Service Manual.

Background

Espoo is a rapidly growing city, with foreign speakers accounting for 20% of the population — a figure forecast to rise past 30% by 2035. The city's strategy places a strong emphasis on immigrant integration in collaboration with the broader Espoo community, and the City Council has set the goal of making Espoo the foremost city for integration in Finland (City of Espoo Integration Programme 2022–2025).

The project was carried out with Trust-M, a research collaboration between Aalto University, the University of Helsinki, Tampere University, and the City of Espoo, aiming to create trustworthy digital public services for migrant integration in Finland. Within our seven-week Designing for Services course, our focus was the integration of stay-at-home mothers and their preferred information channels — a focus that led Trust-M to develop a new Hybrid Service Manual as their next step.

Scope

This project sits mainly within problem definition and design principles. The research initially set out to study the integration process of stay-at-home mothers directly, but reaching them proved difficult, so we expanded the scope to include the service providers working closely with this group. Interviews focused on preferred information channels, and showed that channel use is shaped by personal and cultural factors — most information still travels through family and community.

The final study explored how the Service Manual could improve communication between immigrants and service providers. A co-creation workshop turned these findings into concrete recommendations for the Manual's role in service communication — insights that, despite the shifting scope along the way, carried the project to its conclusions.

Research methodology

We began with desk research, reviewing Trust-M's materials alongside further sources on integration, with a focus on stay-at-home mothers — a group that is harder to reach and uses fewer integration services. From there we conducted four expert interviews with Espoo and NGO workers, plus interviews with immigrants, especially women and mothers, to build a deeper understanding of their day-to-day.

Expert interviews

Five interviews: the head of integration at NGO Monika-Naiset, a communications specialist from Hello Espoo, a senior expert from the mayor's office (also the city's contact person with the Muslim community), and the coordinator of NGO Neighborhood Mothers.

Immigrants, Iso Omena Library

Interviews in the library's reading section and its Language Café — a drop-in group for practising Finnish at any skill level. Eight participants took part, using a visual questionnaire to support the conversation.

Neighborhood Mothers

In-depth interviews with four immigrant women (three of them mothers) through Neighborhood Mothers, using the same materials as the Language Café sessions. A key takeaway: how much the process of finding information varies between women.

Findings

Stay-at-home mothers are falling out of the integration process — their social circles are small, and there is no integration plan built for their situation. One good practice did emerge: a network approach, where whoever first meets a person in need can direct them to Hello Espoo. The rest of the findings are summarised below.

01 Integration is more than just employment

Researching the lives of immigrant women surfaced the softer side of integration. Language skills and employment are the usual yardsticks, but there are other ways people find their place and start to feel at home.

We asked immigrants to rate how integrated they feel (Figure 8). Integration can happen quickly or take years, and setbacks like racism can set it back further. When asked what would help them feel more at home, most people pointed to finding a job or learning the language.

Answers to the question "How integrated do you feel?"

02 Integration services favor job seekers

Stay-at-home mothers face extra hurdles. Small social circles limit participation, and fitting into the standard three-year integration plan is hard with young children — after three years, access to some services simply ends. Integration programmes are largely built around job seekers: 25 hours of weekly study is a high bar for a stay-at-home parent, and the home care allowance quietly reinforces staying home.

03 Face-to-face interaction is important

For sensitive issues like domestic violence, in-person meetings remain essential — phone or chat can't guarantee privacy or confirm who is actually speaking. Meeting in person also lets skilled workers notice hidden problems, and lets immigrants show their own motivation in ways that shape the service they receive.

04 Social contacts are the most important source of information

People reach for very different platforms, but one thing holds across the board: social support matters everywhere. Family, friends and community are the first place people turn when searching for information — especially for those with fewer technical or language skills.

05 Trust is shaped by inadequate knowledge

Missing or outdated information changes the whole service experience. When it isn't clear why a task is required, it feels needlessly hard — and hearing about someone else's experience can set expectations that don't match reality, leaving people feeling unfairly treated.

Digital public service

Trust-M's research team is scoped around an AI-assisted digital public service, which gave our project a new direction: envisioning how our findings could support a more trustworthy immigrant integration service delivered through AI-assisted digital tools. A co-creation workshop was set up to help turn this direction into ideas.

Co-creation workshop

We ran the workshop in Otaniemi at Aalto University — two hours, eight participants: four from Trust-M, three from the City of Espoo, and one AI expert.

Task
  • How would an AI-assisted Digital Service Manual affect their journeys?
  • How would AI influence the value currently provided through human interaction?
  • What would be the best case, and the worst case?

Outcome

Building on our findings and ideas, we set out to give Trust-M something practical to carry into their AI-assisted digital service. The Manual is envisioned as hybrid, with the specific technology still undecided — so alongside the outcome itself, we've shared what we learned along the way.
